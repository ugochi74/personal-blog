# Learn from Mistakes: Developing a Growth Mindset

In **Mindset**, psychologist Carol S. Dweck discusses the difference between a fixed mindset and a growth mindset. A growth mindset involves seeing abilities as something that can be developed through learning, practice, feedback, and effective strategies.

It does not mean that effort alone guarantees success. It means that difficulty can provide information about what to improve.

## Change the questions you ask

Instead of saying, "I am not good at programming," ask, "Which part of this concept do I not understand yet?"

Instead of hiding a mistake, investigate it. Read the error message, reproduce the problem, test a possible fix, and write down the lesson.

Instead of comparing your beginning with someone else's advanced work, compare your current skills with what you could do last month.

## Make feedback useful

Good feedback is specific. "This function needs improvement" is less helpful than "This function handles valid input but does not check for an empty string."

Accepting feedback does not mean accepting every opinion without thought. Consider the evidence, ask questions, and decide what will improve the work.

**Reflection:** Think of one recent mistake. What did it reveal, and what will you try differently next time?
