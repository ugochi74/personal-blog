# The Pragmatic Programmer: Think Like a Problem Solver

**The Pragmatic Programmer** by Andrew Hunt and David Thomas encourages developers to take responsibility for their work, keep learning, and make practical decisions. It is especially useful as a reminder that software engineering is more than writing syntax.

A programmer's job is to understand a problem, choose a suitable solution, communicate clearly, and maintain the result.

## Ideas worth practising

- **Take ownership:** If you notice a problem, investigate it and communicate what needs to happen.
- **Avoid knowledge silos:** Share useful discoveries through documentation and code reviews.
- **Automate repetitive work:** Scripts and reliable tools can reduce mistakes.
- **Learn continuously:** Languages and frameworks change, but problem-solving habits remain valuable.
- **Make changes carefully:** Small, reversible changes are easier to understand and debug.

## Apply the mindset to a project

Before adding a feature, write down what the feature should do. Identify its inputs, expected outputs, and possible failure cases. Implement one small part, test it, and record what you learned.

Do not confuse using a sophisticated tool with solving the right problem. A simple solution that is easy to maintain is often better than a complicated one that is difficult to explain.
